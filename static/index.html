Computer Vision Roadmap

Medical Imaging & Radiograph Analysis Roadmap

Architectural blueprint for integrating deep-learning diagnostic models for chest X-rays and medical radiographs in future versions.

1. Why Text NLP Classifiers Cannot Diagnose X-Rays

Scientific Integrity & Medical Ethics in AI Development

Examiner Note: Our current verified models (Multinomial Naive Bayes and Logistic Regression) operate exclusively on textual n-gram tokens extracted via TF-IDF from written medical documents. Feeding an X-ray image directly into a text classifier is scientifically invalid. In accordance with clinical AI guidelines, we explicitly present a structured engineering roadmap rather than producing fabricated image predictions.

2. Required Vision Architecture

  • Deep CNNs & ViTs: DenseNet-121 (CheXNet architecture), EfficientNet-B4, or Swin Vision Transformers.
  • Validated Benchmarks: Pre-trained on NIH ChestX-ray14 (112,120 frontal views) or Stanford CheXpert (224,316 radiographs).
  • Multi-Label Head: Predicts 14 independent cardiopulmonary conditions (Cardiomegaly, Effusion, Consolidation, Infiltration, Pneumothorax).
  • DICOM Windowing: Preserves 16-bit dynamic range Hounsfield density before resizing.

3. Explainable AI & Saliency Heatmaps

  • Grad-CAM Visualization: Computes the gradient of the predicted abnormality score with respect to feature maps in the final convolutional layer.
  • Radiologist Heatmaps: Highlights the precise anatomical lung zone (e.g. left lower lobe consolidation) triggering the probability.
  • Clinical Verification: Guarantees that the network is focusing on actual pathology rather than hospital markers, pacemaker wires, or border artifacts.

About the Medical AI Assistant Project

Comprehensive overview of the problem statement, objectives, NLP workflow, algorithms, and practical future scope.

1. Problem Statement

Over 1.5 million biomedical papers and millions of diagnostic patient reports are produced annually. Clinicians, students, and patients face heavy cognitive load deciphering complex diagnostic jargon, lab parameters, and research abstracts. Automating text classification and structured report interpretation streamlines clinical documentation and health literacy.

2. Project Objectives

  • Implement clinical-safe text preprocessing without losing medical acronyms or dosages.
  • Multilingual Clinical Understanding: Seamlessly interprets English, Hinglish, and Gujlish patient queries and reports.
  • Extract high-importance unigram & bigram features using statistical TF-IDF.
  • Train and benchmark classical NLP classifiers (Naive Bayes & Logistic Regression).
  • Parse patient laboratory reports, comparing values with printed and clinical reference ranges.
  • Decode ultrasound/sonography radiological findings into clear, plain language with multilingual explanations.
  • Ensure strict evaluation without data leakage using 5-fold CV and held-out testing.

4. Technologies Used

Python 3.10+ Multilingual NLP Engine (Hinglish & Gujlish) FastAPI Scikit-learn PyMuPDF (High-Res Rasterizer) RapidOCR (ONNX OCR Engine) pypdf (PDF Reader) Pillow (Image Processing) Pandas & NumPy Vanilla HTML5/CSS3 Vanilla JavaScript (ES6) Joblib

5. Limitations & Future Scope

  • Multi-label Classification: Extending the text classifier to predict multiple co-morbid disease labels per abstract.
  • Transformer Encoders: Incorporating domain-specific models like BioBERT or PubMedBERT.
  • Computer Vision Integration: Adding CheXNet CNN models with Grad-CAM heatmaps for chest radiographs.
  • OCR Engine: Embedding offline on-device OCR models for handwriting recognition on legacy paper prescriptions.
